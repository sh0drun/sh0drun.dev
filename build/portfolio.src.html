sh0drun is a signature, not a title. The first code was Python inside Blender in 2007, aged thirteen: pasted plugin snippets that cleared a scene, printed the names of objects, moved one of them. I changed the numbers and the strings and watched the viewport change. The first thing built on purpose was a paint program on an HTML canvas, well before university.

Graphics has been the second track ever since. WGFW starts two months into the first job, dtgl in 2021, Atlas in 2026. Demoscene groups have been fitting whole worlds into a few kilobytes for thirty years, and once you have seen that, reaching for an engine to draw three primitives on a checkerboard feels like the wrong instinct.

    Under 64 KB

    Every build of the intro, measured. Raw code and data climb past the 64 KB ceiling; what ships stays at a third of it. The gap between the two lines is the packer.

    Final
    21,731 bytes, 33.6% of the 64 KB budget
    Raw
    67,072 bytes of code and data at its peak, over the ceiling on its own
    Shaders
    50,259 bytes of GLSL after minification, inside that 21 KB
    Packer
    kkrunchy --best --refsize 64, two variants benchmarked per build
    Binary
    32-bit x86 PE, no C runtime
    On disk
    Nothing. Geometry, materials and the soundtrack are all generated at startup

    53 measured builds over nine days. Regressions were tracked to the byte and reverted when they cost more than they saved. One entry in the log reads “noinline on beams helpers: +2 B, reverted”.

    Eight ways to light it

    One sphere, one cube, one torus, raymarched from the same distance field, lit eight different ways, one per bar of the track. Same geometry every time; only the shading model changes.

    Eight stones

    The centre body cycles through eight shapes and materials while the lasers keep firing, so you can watch the same beams bend, split and bounce differently through each one. Refractive indices are the real ones; colour comes from Beer-Lambert absorption rather than a tint.

    Intersection
    Analytic, not marched. Faceted solids are intersected as a set of planes
    Transport
    Reflection and refraction per hit, with total internal reflection at steep angles
    Colour
    Beer-Lambert absorption through the body, so thickness decides saturation
    Beams
    Traced on the CPU, then handed to the shader as segments
    Cost
    Cube and octahedron are the slow presets: more planes, more intersection work

    The engine

    Atlas. Deferred, physically based, written from nothing. No engine, no framework, no rendering library. All three pieces above run on it.

    • Deferred PBR, clustered shading
    • Cascaded, spot and point shadows
    • Image-based lighting
    • SSAO, SSR, SSGI
    • Volumetric light shafts
    • Depth of field, bloom, FXAA
    • Order-independent transparency
    • GPU compute particles
    • SDF raymarching
    • Marching cubes, voxel meshing
    • Procedural texture engine
    • Keyframe director, audio sync
